# Kids News iPhone App — One-Page Brief

> Audience: a designer or non-technical reader who has not seen the web product.
> Companion file: `PROJECT-SUMMARY.md` (deeper, more technical).

## The product in one line

A **daily 21-minute reading habit for kids ages 8–13** — three carefully chosen
news stories, each broken into four short learning steps. Finish them all, and
the day is done.

## Why now, why this

Kids today scroll endlessly through algorithm-driven feeds with no boundary,
no quality filter, and no end-state. Reading muscles atrophy, attention
fragments, parents feel helpless. We replace that with something **finite,
curated, and habit-shaped**: a clear daily session that ends at 21 minutes,
no infinite "next."

## A reader's day

Every morning the app surfaces three stories — one **News**, one **Science**,
one **Fun**. Each story has four short tabs:

1. **Read & Words** — the body, with hard words highlighted and tap-to-define.
2. **Background** — who/what/where/why context the kid might not know.
3. **Quiz** — six multiple-choice questions, instant feedback.
4. **Think** — short discussion prompts, kid types or speaks a response.

Finishing each tab earns minutes toward the day's goal: **2 + 1 + 2 + 2 = 7
per story.** Three stories × 7 = **21 minutes done.** No more content waiting.
The app celebrates and steps back until tomorrow.

## What makes this different from existing kids' apps

- **A real boundary.** No infinite scroll. No "next." When the 21 minutes are
  done, the home screen says "see you tomorrow."
- **Two reading levels per story.** Sprout (4th-grade prose) and Tree (7th-grade
  prose) of the same story. The reader picks once; the whole app adapts.
- **The streak is the long game.** A 21-day streak is the brand promise.
  No coins, no shop, no leaderboards. Just "show up every day."
- **No accounts, no ads, no comments.** Kids don't need a feed; parents
  don't need to worry about data or strangers.
- **Real publishers, AI-rewritten.** Stories come from PBS, NPR, IEEE,
  Smithsonian, etc. Mined every morning, rewritten in age-appropriate
  language, hand-vetted for safety. Not user-generated, not ad-funded.

## What we want from the iPhone app design pass

The web product proves the daily-21 model works. The iPhone app is the
version **kids actually use** — native, tactile, quietly delightful.
Five surfaces deserve craft:

1. **The "Today's 21 minutes" hero.** A single, clear arc that fills as the
   kid finishes steps. This is the soul of the app — make it feel like
   showing up to a friend, not a homework screen.
2. **The four-tab article flow.** Native iOS lets us add gesture transitions,
   subtle haptics on each step finish, a confident "you got it" feel that
   the web can't match.
3. **The streak ring + day calendar.** Where the kid sees "I came back 14
   days in a row." Worth designing carefully — it's the retention engine.
4. **First-launch onboarding.** One screen, no signup, no email. Pick a
   reading level, optional name, today's three stories appear. That's it.
5. **The bookend states.** `0 / 21 today` and `21 / 21 — see you tomorrow.`
   Most apps neglect both; both should make a kid feel something.
